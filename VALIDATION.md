## Follow-up: floating icons left after a drag

The palette previously only finished a drag on its button's `pointerup` or `pointercancel`. Lost capture or focus could leave both its fixed-position ghost and the map's placement preview active. ## ENSO climate core, stage 1 (`src/simulation/climate-core.ts`, `tests/climate-core.test.ts`)

Anomaly-only 1-D equatorial Pacific (Kelvin + Rossby n=1 + slow off-equatorial Rossby, 2.5 degree cells, 120E-280E, daily step) coupled to an SST-anomaly equation and a steady Matsuno-Gill atmosphere, with state-dependent westerly wind bursts and player SST patches. Stage 2 (below) wires it into the weather engine. `npx vitest run tests/climate-core.test.ts` runs 20 seeds x 200 yr plus the twin experiments (about 22 s); observed values come from `tests/fixtures/enso-observed.json` (ERSST v5 Nino 3.4, 1950-2024). No integral controller remains.

Structure: convective heating is a convex function of total SST (zero below 26.27 C, slope 1 between 29.28 and 30.55 C, saturating at 33.57 C), so the cold tongue heats the atmosphere only when an anomaly lifts it over the threshold and very warm water stops adding heating; burst rate and speed grow with Nino 4 and burst centres follow the 28.5 C warm-pool edge (same random numbers in twins, so twins differ only through physics); a slow Rossby mode (c/7, carrying 77% of the Rossby forcing) re-enters the equator at the western wall and supplies the slow recharge time scale that the n=1 wave cannot. In stochastic runs the climatological mean of the nonlinear terms (asymmetric Tsub, convex heating, bursts) is removed analytically; noise-free runs have no bursts and none of these corrections, so T = 0 is their equilibrium.

Saturation: h = K + R is limited to -145..-42 m (shoaling, following a 170 m to 50 m mean thermocline, 85% of it) and +120 m (deep); each wave amplitude is limited to 150 m; total SST to 18..33.5 C; the forcing surface wind to 25 m/s; convective heating anomalies to 4 nondimensional units (about 20 mm/day). Overlapping patches add up before these limits. Player bursts take at most 16 of 24 slots, leaving 8 for natural bursts, and a player may apply at most 4 thermocline pulses in 30 days. In the free run h stays between -44 m and +100 m, inside the limits; the eastern shoaling limit (about -43 m) is close to the free-run minimum.

Limits and compensations that remain, all named constants in the source:
- Skewness (+0.9 to +1.3) is about 2.5 times the observed value, big events are about 1.9 times too frequent, and La Nina peaks are too weak (-0.93 vs -1.15). `TSUB_NEG` is near the bottom of its tuning range (1.41 C).

- Eastern reflection is low (0.39): at 0.8 the model with bursts replaced by their expectation no longer decays from +2.5 C.
- `SEASON_AMP` 0.785 is large (upwelling varies by a factor of about 8 through the year); at 0.5 the phase-locking median falls to 0.49.
- `GILL_STRESS_EFF` 0.549 scales the wind the ocean feels below the Gill wind.
- `WALKER_COMP` 2.25 is not a small fraction: heating west of 160E is reduced by 2.25 times the mean heating anomaly of 160E-280E (about 75% of what a mass-balanced cell would need, 3). During warm events it can turn heating west of 160E negative even where the local SST anomaly is warm, and it produces the large Darwin response in D2 (+5.3 hPa).
- The Gill atmosphere uses y and x in units of (c/2beta)^0.5 = 1045 km, so the response is narrower than the earlier 1470 km scale.
- First-order upwind advection at CFL 0.7 is numerically diffusive: the modified-equation estimate is a 12-17% amplitude loss over 90 degrees for packets of half-width 12-15 degrees.
- The 365-day calendar has no leap years; a model year is 365 days of the 12 monthly normals.
- The rectification corrections assume Gaussian anomalies with fixed standard deviations; they remove the drift but are not a controller.
- Off-equatorial patches project onto the symmetric Gill modes only, so they produce twin gyres rather than a single-hemisphere response.
- The constants were found by a search scoring these same acceptance tests on seeds 1-20 (twin seeds 1-5 and 1-10); seeds 41-60 and twin seeds 41-50 were not used.

Attribution, one mechanism at a time (same constants): removing the state dependence of bursts gives std 0.73-0.83 and 14/20 on the spectrum test, with 8.9 big events per century; replacing the convective threshold with linear heating makes the model strongly supercritical (std 3.7-3.8).

## ENSO climate layer, stage 2 (`src/simulation/climate-coupling.ts`, `tests/climate-coupling.test.ts`)

The stage-1 core now drives the weather. `GameState.climateLayer` (null until activated) holds the core, a smoothed Nino 3.4 (45-day exponential mean, about a 90-day running mean) and the current 2 degree anomaly fields. It is activated by the ENSO slider leaving 0, by a warm or cool tool within 30 degrees of the equator (not by formation recipes), or by a live-weather start; an untouched base world has no layer and is bit-for-bit what it was (`tests/fixtures/base-world-golden.json`, recorded from the commit before this stage, 200 steps across two day boundaries). The layer is derived from seed, start day, actions and the starting analysis, so replay files store nothing for it. `REPLAY_FILE_VERSION` is 7; version 6 files are still read when they would never build a layer (no ENSO slider off 0, no non-recipe warm/cool tool within 30 degrees, no starting analysis) and rejected with an explanation otherwise. A base-world layer is deterministic (no natural bursts or noise), so a tool or a released slider starts no random ENSO event; a live layer keeps the natural noise.

Once per model day (every 96 steps) the layer advances one climate day and rebuilds one set of anomalies (SST, air temperature, humidity, pressure, direct tropical wind, geostrophic wind of the pressure anomaly, shear). The grid, the point sampler, `totalWind`/`totalPressure` and storm steering read it; the grid folds it into per-day effective base arrays, so nothing else runs per step. The set is stored on the layer, so a structured clone carries it to the main thread with nothing to rebuild and no cache that two states could share.

Mapping. Tropics: the core's Gill fields (SST, SLP, wind, 0.01 humidity per mm/day of rain anomaly, clamped to 0.15). Extratropics (faded in from 25 to 35 degrees), land air temperature, the non-Pacific tropics, humidity wherever the model's own rain anomaly is small, and the 200-850 hPa shear at all latitudes: the NCEP/NCAR regression maps times the change of the smoothed Nino 3.4 (`CLIMATE_SOURCE` labels the two for stage 3). The Gill baroclinic shear change (-2u, -2v) is kept only close to a player patch. Ocean air follows 0.7 of the SST anomaly in a base world (a live game's air already relaxes toward its sea temperature). Everything is a change since the layer started. In a live game the pressure anomaly joins the equilibrium of `stepLiveWeather`, the climate SST and air anomalies are left out of its thermal low, and the tropical wind (faded out between 8 and 20 degrees) joins the wind target. Where a historic reanalysis window is active the layer's anomalies are not applied (grid, sampler, `totalWind`, `totalPressure` and steering). The ENSO slider is an imposed experiment that shows at once, paused or running: `setSstHold` sets the equatorial SST straight to the held pattern (flat across the 190-240E box, Gaussian taper of 20 degrees outside it, so there is no sharp front), the smoothed Nino 3.4 that scales the remote maps jumps to the held value, the anomalies and the grid are rebuilt and the state is posted. The atmosphere (steady Gill) follows in the same rebuild; in a live world the pressure and wind of the evolving weather are also moved by the change of the layer's anomaly, because they would otherwise relax over 72 h. A 10-day nudge keeps the pattern held; only the thermocline adjusts over weeks. Returning the slider to 0 releases the hold and the ocean evolves from where it is (noise-free core: largest |Nino 3.4| in year 40 under 1e-7). On the overlay data path (grid through the renderer's field sampler), base / live GFS fixture, La Nina -2 vs a control, paused: 150W sea -1.8 / -4.0 C, Darwin pressure -0.6 / -4.0 hPa, Tahiti +0.3 / +3.4 hPa (`tests/climate-overlay.test.ts`). Before: SST moved 1/10 of the gap per day (+0.35 C at 150W after two days) and nothing changed while paused; a live world's pressure relaxed over 72 h. Overlapping same-sign SST patches merge (`stackSstPatch`: anomalies add up to 6 C, areas add, the hold is the longest of the merged durations), which is what turns stacked tools into a basin-wide event. A tool's own warm/cool water and the layer's SST patch are a union (the larger magnitude wins), so a single tool still shows exactly its old centre SST. The layer calendar follows the world calendar (and `setSeason`); while a historic typhoon runs, the base seasons follow the typhoon's own dates but the layer, which is not applied inside the record window, keeps the world's.

## Stage 3: one speed control and conditions that drive the climate (`src/simulation/climate-core.ts`, `climate-coupling.ts`, `engine.ts`, `worker.ts`; `tests/climate-fast-path.test.ts`, `tests/climate-conditions.test.ts`)

The separate Climate Lab (panel, mode switch, cause forms, control twin) was removed. The climate now runs in the normal game screen.

**Fast path.** `TIME_SPEEDS` has one more step, 1 month/s (2 592 000); keys 1-9 select the nine steps. Up to 2 days/s the weather engine steps as before. Above it the worker switches by itself (`setClimateMode`, recorded as `climate-mode`) and advances the ENSO layer by whole model days (`advanceClimateTime`, recorded and merged as `climate-advance`). The first advance of a stay ends storms, remnants, candidates, thunderstorms, hazards and tools without damage (one news item, translated); tools placed later stay, age by the days and end. Genesis does not run until the speed is back down. The calendar is `calendarOffsetDays = total fast days x 365.25 / 365`, a pure function of the day total, so chunking cannot change it; the layer (365-day year) and the world (365.25) return to the same date every year. Leaving realigns the layer with the world date. Entering gives the layer natural variability; a world that never enters stays noise-free. The worker rebuilds anomalies and grid (`refreshClimateView`, display only) once per 250 ms tick and posts the state; live and replay differ only in how often that ran, and the replay test (`replays identically whatever the view rebuild cadence`) shows the core, calendar, scores and sampled environments equal. Run limit: 200 fast years. The clock shows the year after a year or on the fast path (`2031년 3월`).

Cost (node, one core, per real second): a model day costs 8.7 us; a view rebuild (anomalies + grid) 5.5 ms, four per second. 1 month/s: 22 ms (2.2 %). The 200 ms tick budget bounds the day loop.

**Conditions drive the climate.** `addHeatPatch` / `stackHeatPatch` (16 slots, serialized, validated: radius 2-40, |strength| <= 9, 1-730 days, within 30 degrees) heat or cool the Gill atmosphere, projected onto e^{-y^2/4} at 1 degree spacing. They have no ocean footprint, fade with the atmosphere's adjustment time (0.242/GILL_EPS = 2.4 days) after the source ends, and stack like SST patches (same sign, overlapping: strengths add to 9, areas add, longest duration). Strengths (`HEAT_PER_TOOL`): low +2, updraft +1.4, warm air +1, unstable air +0.8; high -2, downdraft -1.4, cold air -1, stable air -0.8. Moisture and dry air feed nothing: the core's heating depends on sea temperature, and humidity alone releases no heat without lifting (the weather layer already says moisture cannot create a storm alone). Radius and duration are each tool's own. Formation recipes are not fed. Inside the footprint (exp(-(d/r)^2), the shape of the tool's own Gaussian) of the patches of low and high (the only tools with a pressure system of their own; the patch carries a serialized `heatOwn` tag, and only same-kind patches merge) the layer's Gill pressure of those patches is subtracted (full solution minus the solution without them), so a tool's own pressure is not added twice; wind, rain and remote effects stay, and an updraft keeps its Gill pressure (-0.87 hPa at the centre of six updrafts against -0.04 / +0.03 for six lows / highs). The exclusion ends when the tool does. With all 16 slots in use a new patch takes the weakest fading slot or merges into the nearest same-sign, same-kind patch. `REPLAY_FILE_VERSION` is 8; version 6 and 7 files are read only when `replayActivatesClimateLayer` is false (the layer's physics changed after version 7).

## Stage 4: historic climate scenarios (`src/simulation/climate-scenarios.ts`, `climate-core.ts`, `engine.ts`; `tests/climate-scenario.test.ts`, `tests/browser/climate-scenario.spec.ts`)

A player loads **1997-98 El Nino** or **2010-11 La Nina** from the Climate experience tab of the palette (a tile each, like the Typhoon experience tab; press the loaded tile again to switch it off) and watches it on the normal map, speed control and Measure tab. No separate panel or mode.

**Engine.** `loadClimateScenario(state, id)` is recorded as the replay action `climate-scenario` (validated: unknown ids are rejected; `REPLAY_FILE_VERSION` stays 8 because nothing a version 8 log could already contain changes, and version 6 and 7 logs containing it are refused like any other layer-building log). It ends storms, remnants, forming lows, thunderstorms, hazards and tools without damage and drops a live analysis (one translated news line, no extra one), sets the ENSO slider to 0, replaces the layer with a noise-free one, and moves the calendar to 1 January of the first year through `startDayOfYear` (the mechanism of `setSeason`; elapsed model time, and so every Measure line, stays continuous). `GameState.climateScenario` (only present after a load, so other worlds are unchanged) carries the year for the clock; the layer's own year is set too. The scripted forcing is `ClimateState.script`, entries `[day, kind, a, b, c]` (kind 0 wind burst, kind 1 trade-wind anomaly), serialised with the core, validated by `restoreClimate` (multiple of five, at most 64 entries, day, kind, burst and trade ranges; states serialised before scenarios restore with an empty script) and applied by `stepClimateDay` at the start of its day. A scenario layer is noise-free while its script runs. On the fast path, the first model day after the last script entry (`scriptEndDay`: the last day of a burst, or the day of a trade-wind entry; day 600 for 1997, 810 for 2010) `advanceClimateLayer` switches natural variability on through the existing serialize/restore rebuild; the condition is a function of the core's day only, so it is identical live, in replay and whatever the chunking (tested with 30- and 25-day chunks and a replay). At weather speeds the layer stays noise-free, as every layer does until the fast path is entered. The 1997 script ends with a trade-wind-to-0 entry on day 600, because switching noise on in mid-event (day 177, after the last burst) wrecked the event (DJF Nino 3 fell to -0.6). After the script the slider (`setSstHold`) and conditions act on the layer as on any layer, and a 96-month run after each scenario varies between about -1.6 and +3.4 C. Pressing a season while a scenario is loaded ends it (`setSeason` -> `endClimateScenario`: the script, the trade-wind anomaly and `climateScenario` are cleared, the ocean keeps its state, the clock returns to its normal format, one translated news line; deterministic in replay), because the date move would desynchronise the script and the Observed line. `ClimateScenarioInfo.loads` counts loads (from the action log), so loading the same scenario twice restarts the Measure line and gets distinct news ids. Shared physics constants were not touched.

**Inputs (approximate dates and strengths, after the sources named in the code).** 1997: recharged ocean (zonal-mean thermocline +10 m) and the burst sequence of the existing hindcast test (day 0 150E 6 m/s 12 d; 57 155E 7 m/s 15 d; 124 160E 8 m/s 20 d; 164 165E 6 m/s 12 d; McPhaden 1999, Science 283, 950). 2010: discharged ocean (-15 m), the decaying El Nino left as a warm start (Nino 3.4 +1.5 C), and a basin-wide trade-wind anomaly rising from 0.5 m/s on day 90 to 1.7-1.8 m/s (days 210-450), easing to 0 on day 810 (NOAA CPC monitoring of the 2010-11 event; Boening et al. 2012, GRL 39, L19602). The trade-wind values were chosen with this model, not measured: they are a scripted cause that gives roughly the observed size of the La Nina.

The acceptance cases are labelled 'fast path, one day at a time' (the 14-day chunked equivalence is its own test). The acceptance numbers above did not move.

**Observed series.** `scripts/prepare-climate.mjs` now also writes `src/data/generated/nino34-observed.json` (ERSST v5 Nino 3.4 monthly, 1950-2024, 5 kB, same numbers as the test fixture; the script reruns from its cache and leaves the normals and fixture byte-identical); `src/data/climate-observed.ts` reads it. Calendar jump: the model's elapsed time does not jump, so a chart open before the load keeps its place; its recorded lines are cleared at the load (they belong to the old climate) and start again from the load, and the observed months are laid out from that time. **Limits.** Dates and strengths are approximate and the model reproduces the causes, not the exact event; Help (Climate tab, Scenarios) says so in all five languages. The model overshoots 1997 (about 3.2 C at the peak against 2.4 C) and its La Nina is milder than 1998-99 (-0.9 against -1.5 after the 1997 event). The 2010 start does not reproduce the Jan-Apr 2010 decay rate (model Jan-Apr monthly 0.64, 0.35, 0.22, 0.12 against observed 1.53, 1.26, 0.91, 0.39: the discharged ocean removes the warmth in weeks). After the 2010 script the model does not hold the 2011 double dip: a strong trade-wind anomaly through 2011 still gives El Nino-like warming in late 2011, and when the trades end the free model rebounds to an El Nino of about +3 C around 2012 (the observed 2012 was neutral). This is the model's short cycle (2-3 years) and its excess of big El Ninos (stage 1 limits), not something the scenario can script away without changing the shared physics. At weather speeds a scenario layer has no natural noise, so the years after the script are a deterministic ring-down; on the fast path they are a random future.

## Stage 5: events of a loaded scenario (`src/simulation/climate-events.ts`, `engine.ts`; `tests/climate-events.test.ts`)

Everything here is active only while a climate scenario is loaded (`state.climateScenario`); a plain world pays one property check per model day (about 1 ns) and `base-world-golden.json` is unchanged. `REPLAY_FILE_VERSION` stays 8: the new behaviour only exists in worlds that contain a `climate-scenario` action, which no earlier release could write.

**Typhoons.** The load turns Natural typhoon formation on (as `setGenesis(true)`, but as part of the `climate-scenario` action, so no extra action is recorded). The previous setting is kept in `climateScenario.priorGenesis` and restored when the scenario is switched off or a season change ends it. A genesis switch made by the player during the scenario replaces the saved value, and loading a second scenario keeps the value from before the first. The UI sets 4h/s on load (`SCENARIO_TIME_SPEED`); the player may change it, and at 7d/s the weather is not calculated, so no storm or climate event forms. Genesis waves' own conditions (negative ids) are not re-fed into the layer when a scenario is switched off.

**Climate-driven disasters** (`stepClimateEvents`, once per model day from `stepGame`, so weather speeds only; the fast path never runs it). For each of 16 regions: the layer's smoothed Nino 3.4 must be at least 0.7 C on the region's side of zero, the date must be in the region's season, and the layer's local anomaly at the region's probe point must pass `lo`; the daily probability is `0.04 * clamp((anomaly - lo) / (hi - lo), 0, 1)`, drawn with `hash01(seed ^ loads * 7919, day, 31000 + regionIndex)`. A region then rests 35 model days and at most 4 climate-driven hazards run at once (the order of the regions rotates by day so the cap does not favour one). The event is a normal `WeatherHazard` (flag `climate`), so the disaster record, damage, vehicles and news come from the existing systems. Kinds used: heavy rain is **downpour** (3 x its 12 h = 36 h), drought is **drought** (2 x 120 h) and heat is **heatwave** (2 x 72 h); the news title reads, for example, "El Niño: drought in Indonesia" and carries the place, which a click shows on the map.

The thresholds are in the layer's own units (relative humidity as a fraction, degrees C) and sit just under the strongest anomaly the layer gives each place in a strong event (probed over both scenarios on a 2-degree grid in each region and season). ## Stage 6: fine dust, PM2.5 (`src/simulation/fine-dust.ts`, `src/render/dust-hexes.ts`, `engine.ts`; `tests/fine-dust.test.ts`, `tests/browser/fine-dust.spec.ts`)

**Model.** A concentration field `cell.pm25` (µg/m³) on the 2° weather grid, stepped once per 15 min step after the grid refresh, so it uses the model's actual surface wind. Semi-Lagrangian advection (bilinear, so it is stable at every speed and wind, never negative, never above its neighbours), 4 % neighbour diffusion, then an implicit source and sink step. Source: populated cells inside a Fine dust event (formation tool, anchor High, 96 h, radius 600 km), `6 µg/m³/h x (pop / 2e7)^0.6` weighted by the event footprint and a 12 h build-up, divided by a mixing-layer factor (`(1 + 0.5 instability)` clamped 0.35..2.5, times `1 - (p - 1013) / 50` clamped 0.5..1.5): stable air and high pressure make it shallow, so it piles up. Sinks (1/h): `0.004 + 0.012 humidity` deposition (slower in dry air), `0.002 max(0, wind - 6)^1.3` wind dispersal, `0.05 max(0, instability)`, and `min(0.5, 0.04 rain)` washout. Capped at 600. Open water has no population, so it emits nothing. It runs only while an event exists or haze is left (`state.fineDustRows`, the row band that holds dust); a plain world skips it, so `base-world-golden.json` is unchanged. The fast climate path (7d/s) does not step the weather, so the field is frozen with the rest of the world and resumes when you slow down. Deterministic, no random draws; no replay change (the new formation tag is a valid weather kind, so `REPLAY_FILE_VERSION` stays).

**Gameplay.** Formation tool `Fine dust formation` (ko 미세먼지 발생), key `'` (physical `Quote` fallback), needs a cell of at least 500,000 people; recipe: High until 1022 hPa plus Stable air. Health impact on populated cells with PM2.5 of 35.5 or more (`0.02 x smoothstep(35.5, 250, pm25)`), through the usual records, score and city bulletins; no deaths, missing or flooded homes. News: "Fine dust: very unhealthy air in Seoul". Bands (EPA, unhealthy-for-sensitive folded in): good up to 12, moderate up to 35, unhealthy up to 150, very unhealthy up to 250, hazardous above.

**Rendering.** Fine dust and Yellow dust are drawn as a vector grid of 2° pointy-top hexagons on the overlay canvas (flat and globe), built once per state and only projected per frame. Fine dust: solid grey-brown, 5 levels at the band edges (shown from 12 µg/m³). Yellow dust: dashed sand-yellow with a centre dot (shown from 12 % of its footprint load). Legend rows for both, a PM2.5 value in the hover readout, and a `Fine dust` entry in the Map overlays row. Measure: item 11, `Fine dust (PM2.5)` (ko 미세먼지 농도), no shortcut, band lines at 12, 35.5, 150.5, 250.5, 0 where there is no dust. Yellow dust has no concentration field (only a footprint), so it is not a second plot there.
